01The situation

Open on their problem in their words, with the number that made it a problem. A reader who does not recognise their own situation in this paragraph stops here.

What it was costing, and what they had already tried. Attribute the figures to the client’s own records — their release log, their dashboard — and say which. A case study is proof, and proof has a source.

Then the constraint that shaped the work: the deadline that could not move, the freeze that was not available, the team that could not be staffed. The constraint is what makes the story worth reading.

02What we did

The approach, in plain sequence. Name the decision that mattered and why it was not the obvious one — that is the only part a competitor cannot copy from your website, and it is what a reader is here for.

Keep the mechanism concrete: what ran in parallel, what was rehearsed, what was reversible. Vague competence reads as no competence.

What went wrong

Say it. One or two things that did not go to plan, what they cost, and what changed as a result. A case study with no friction reads as marketing, and buyers say so; this section is the one that makes the rest believable.

03Where it left them

The result, measured the same way as the opening problem, so the two can be compared. If the before was releases per month, the after is releases per month — not a percentage, and not a different metric that happens to look better.

Then what they own now and what they do not need you for. An engagement that leaves a dependency is not a case study, it is a retainer pitch.

  • What they hold: the artefact, and who on their side revises it.
  • What was retired, and when they retired it themselves.
  • What you are no longer on the hook for.
