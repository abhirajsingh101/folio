SECTION 01What you asked for

State the client’s situation in their words, from the call. Getting this paragraph wrong is not recoverable further down: a reader who does not see their own problem in the opening stops reading the price.

Two or three paragraphs on what is happening now and what it costs — the constraint, the deadline, and what has already been tried. Quote the numbers they gave you and attribute them; do not go looking for your own.

Then one paragraph on what changes if this is done, in the same terms. No adjectives: a decision-maker discounts every one of them.

Close by saying what you understand the decision to be, and by when — a proposal that never names the decision leaves the reader to work out what they are being asked for.

SECTION 02Scope

Say what is in and what is out on the same page. Out-of-scope is the half that prevents the argument, so it is not an appendix.

Included

  • Schema extraction and a reversible migration path.
  • Dual-write period with reconciliation reporting.
  • Cutover rehearsal against a production-sized copy.
  • Runbook and handover to your on-call team.

Not included

  • Application changes beyond the data layer.
  • Licence or hosting costs, which you hold directly.
  • Ongoing operation after handover.
