SECTION 01Executive summary

Q3 was the quarter the decomposition stopped being a plan. Twenty-seven of forty-three services now run independently of the monolith, deploy frequency more than doubled, and p99 latency fell 44% against the Q2 baseline.

27/43Services migratedup from 11 in Q2
2,694Deploys+118% quarter on quarter

99.97%Availabilitytwo minor incidents

Three things drove the quarter. The dual-write cutover in July removed the last synchronous dependency on the monolith's write path, which unblocked eight services at once. The shared platform SDK cut the median migration from eleven days to four. And retiring the legacy scheduler removed the single largest source of tail latency.

On track

The programme is one service ahead of the Q3 commitment and holds a two-week buffer into Q4. No milestone slipped.

SECTION 04The path to Q4

Q4 is sequenced around one structural constraint: four of the nine in-flight services share the same write lock on the orders table. They cannot be cut over independently, and no amount of parallel staffing changes that.

Keystone

The orders write lock. checkout-orchestrator, inventory-ledger, pricing-engine and settlement-batch all contend on it. Partitioning it is the single prerequisite for 45% of remaining traffic, so it is sequenced first regardless of individual priority.

Table 3Q4 sequencing
#WorkstreamPriorityDepends onRationale

Sequencing

  1. Split the lock first. Partitioning by tenant is the smallest change that decouples all four contenders; a full event-sourcing rewrite is not required and should not be attempted this quarter.
  2. Run the orphan review in parallel. Nothing depends on it, and the expected outcome is two deletions rather than two migrations.
  3. Cut over by traffic, not by ease. Taking checkout-orchestrator first is unpleasant and correct.
  4. Change the metric. Report traffic share from Q4 onward; service count has stopped being informative.

Migration contract

// every service must satisfy these before it is called migrated
interface MigrationContract {
  ownership:  'named team, on-call rotation, runbook';
  data:       'owns its datastore, no cross-service writes';
  deploy:     'ships without a monolith release';
  contract:   'consumer tests green in CI, versioned API';
  observability: 'traces, SLO, dashboard, alert routing';
  rollback:   'dual-read verified, cutover reversible for 14 days';
}

SECTION 05Risks

Risk

Single-threaded keystone. The lock partition has one engineer with deep context. If that person is unavailable for two weeks, Q4 slips wholesale. Mitigation: a second engineer pairs on it from week one, at a real cost to workstream 4.

Watch

Contract test flake. Suite runtime grew 38% this quarter and three tests now fail intermittently. Teams have started re-running rather than investigating, which is how a gate stops being a gate.

Note

Deliberately out of scope. The reporting database and the internal admin console are not being migrated. Both are scheduled for replacement in H1 and migrating them would be wasted work.

What "done" means for Q4

  • Orders write lock partitioned, with all four contenders cutting over independently.
  • At least 60% of production traffic served by migrated services.
  • No service in the programme without a named owner.
  • Every migrated service satisfies all six clauses of the migration contract.
