SECTION 01Findings

Nitrate exceeded the 11.3 mg/l NO₃-N drinking water standard at four of the fourteen sites, all four below the Marchford confluence, and all four only between February and April.

14Sites monthly, 12 rounds
168Samples of 168 scheduled
4Sites over standard nitrate, spring only
31% Turbidity rise vs 2021–25 mean

The seasonal pattern is the finding, not the exceedances themselves. Nitrate at the four affected sites tracks the spring fertiliser window with a lag of about three weeks, and returns below standard by May in every case. A survey reporting annual means would have shown nothing at all: the annual mean at the worst site, C-09, is 8.4 mg/l.

Not a trend

One hydrological year cannot establish a trend. The comparison against 2021–25 is a comparison of like months against a five-year mean, and the 2026–27 winter was the second wettest in that period — which raises turbidity on its own.

SECTION 02Method, and what it cannot tell you

Turbidity

Holding
Chilled, analysed within 24 h
Lab
UKAS 4471, blind duplicates at 10%

Sites were chosen to bracket the four tributary confluences and the two consented discharges, not to be representative of the catchment as a whole. Three sites (C-02, C-05, C-11) sit within 200 m of a road bridge, which is a known access convenience and a known bias.

What a monthly grab sample cannot tell you

A grab sample is a point in time. Storm-driven peaks last hours, and the probability of a monthly sample catching one is low: on the two occasions a round coincided with a spate, turbidity was between four and nine times the same site's median. Those two rounds are reported and excluded from the seasonal figures, and they are the reason the turbidity comparison is given as a range rather than a mean.

  • No continuous monitoring; no sonde deployed this year.
  • No biological sampling — invertebrate indices would answer a different question and are outside this survey's consent.
  • Nothing here apportions source. Nitrate below a confluence is not evidence about any single landholding.
Do not cite

This survey does not establish compliance or breach by any operator, and must not be quoted in that form. It is a screening survey: it says where to look next.

SECTION 04Recommendations

  1. Deploy a sonde at C-09 for one year. The monthly grab cannot see storm peaks, and C-09 is where they would matter. Approximately £4,200 including servicing.
  2. Add two sites between C-07 and C-08. The step change happens across 4.1 km with three inputs in it; the survey currently cannot say which.
  3. Repeat the spring window at higher frequency. Fortnightly from February to May at the four affected sites, rather than monthly across all fourteen — the same laboratory cost, aimed at the question.
  4. Do not extend the survey to biological indices yet. They answer a different question and would delay the sonde by a season.
