01What we found

The building takes about two thousand visitors a day and half of them come in through the north entrance, which is the one entrance with no sight line to the main stair. The problem is not a shortage of signs. It is that the visitor makes their first decision before they reach one.

We visited three times, at opening, at four o’clock and on a Saturday, and watched where people stopped. They stop in the same place every time: six metres inside the north entrance, where the corridor splits three ways and nothing tells them which way is which. Everything in this proposal is built around fixing that junction first and letting the rest of the system follow from it.

What the fee covers

  • A movement study of the whole building, and a map of the points where a visitor has to decide something.
  • The sign system: hierarchy, wording, materials, sizes and positions.
  • An implementation manual the council keeps, so a new sign five years from now still belongs to the same system.

What it does not

  • Fabrication and installation. Both go to a contractor you appoint, and we will help you brief them.
  • A third language. Quoted separately once the sign schedule is fixed, because the cost depends on how many signs survive the study.

02Programme

The stages run in sequence, and none starts before the one before it is signed off. Twenty weeks from signature, excluding your review periods.

  • Site visits Complete · February 2026
    Three visits at different times of day, and the movement study that produced this proposal.
  • Stage 1 — Study 4 weeks
    Decision-point map, proposed information hierarchy, and a short report you sign off before we draw anything.
  • Stage 2 — Design 10 weeks
    The signs themselves: sizes, positions, materials, and a full-size printed mock-up of the three that matter most.
  • Stage 3 — Manual 6 weeks
    Production files and the manual that lets your team add a sign without coming back to us.
Review time

The programme assumes each stage is reviewed within five working days. Days beyond that are added to the end date as they fall.

04Terms

  1. Ownership. The design transfers to the council on payment of the final invoice. We keep the right to show the work.
  2. Revisions. Two rounds are included in each stage. Anything beyond that is quoted and agreed in writing before we start it.
  3. Payment. Thirty days from invoice. Work on the next stage pauses if an invoice runs more than fifteen days late.
  4. Ending it. Either side may end the engagement with two weeks’ written notice, and what has been done to that date is payable.
Not included

Fabrication, installation and planning consent are outside this fee. Each can be quoted separately once the design is signed off — not before, because the number would be a guess.
