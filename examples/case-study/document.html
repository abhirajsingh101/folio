Ten weeks to get a schema out of the order path

Meridian could not freeze releases and could not keep waiting for schema windows. We took the second problem away without creating the first.

01The situation

Twenty-seven of Meridian's forty-three services were already off the monolith. The programme had stalled anyway, because everything left shared one orders schema, and every change to it needed a window that three teams had to agree on.

By September those windows were holding eleven releases a month — one in three. The cost was not the waiting itself so much as what teams did about it: changes were batched to fit a window, so each release carried more, and the ones that failed were harder to attribute. Peak season started on 24 November and a freeze was not available.

Meridian's platform team knew what needed doing and said plainly that they could not staff it alongside the remaining sixteen service moves. That is the whole brief: take the schema, leave the services.

02What we did

The extraction itself was unremarkable, which was the point. Nine of the forty-one tables had writers outside the order path; those went behind an API first, with shadow traffic compared for seventy-two hours before anything was believed. Dual-write followed, and a reconciliation report that a human could read every morning — not a dashboard, a list of divergences with the row attached.

The decision that mattered was making the cutover reversible for a fortnight rather than a single night. It cost two weeks of dual-write we would rather not have paid for, and it meant nobody had to be brave at three in the morning. The switch was two config changes; the rollback was the same two in reverse, timed at four minutes in rehearsal.

We'd been told before that a migration would be zero-downtime. This is the first time someone showed us the rollback and made us run it ourselves. — Principal engineer, Meridian Platform Engineering

What went wrong

Two things, both in discovery. The schema export we priced against was a week old, and by the time we started there were forty-three tables, not forty-one — small, but we quoted the change rather than absorbing it, which is the habit that keeps the last invoice boring. The second rehearsal also found that the writer queue took 140 seconds to drain, not 90, so the procedure's threshold was wrong before it was ever used in anger. It is 180 seconds now.

03Where it left them

Meridian ran the cutover themselves, from a runbook they had already rehearsed unaided — the last deliverable, and the one that decides whether an engagement leaves anything behind. Thirty-four minutes, no downtime, and the old instance retired three weeks later on their own schedule.

Releases held for a schema window went from eleven in September to one in February, and that one was a genuine coordination, not a queue. The sixteen remaining service moves are back with the platform team, unblocked, which is where they wanted them.
